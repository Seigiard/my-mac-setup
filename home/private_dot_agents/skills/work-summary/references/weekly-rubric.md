# Weekly Update Rubric

How a weekly update is judged, by the manager and by a reviewing agent. Each criterion scores 1–5 with quoted evidence. Reached from `weekly-format.md` when the user asks to check or compare drafts.

1. **Achievements, not process**: every sentence says what is now true for a user, a partner, an operator, or a bet. A 1 has the writer's own tests, things ruled out, "likely", "could not reproduce", or ratios of done to planned ("eight of nine"). A 5 has none of that and no caveat that does not change the reader's conclusion.
2. **Three-minute read**: 500–700 words of prose. 5 = inside the range; 3 = up to 900; 1 = over 1,200 or under 400.
3. **Links once, meaning something**: a link at the sentence it proves, link text saying what opens, one source line under a table. The same link repeated in table rows, a run of PR numbers, or a links row caps the score at 2.
4. **Order**: In short, then the bets, then What's next, then What changed for users, then one line for the rest. In short is a list: one bullet per bet in the body's order, each naming the bet's goal, then one bullet on changes. A bet block carries only the bet's progress; a defect found on the way is in the changelog. 1 = What's next after the changelog, or fixes inside a bet block, or an unstructured In short.
5. **Same facts everywhere**: a number or a date is stated once in the body and matches the summary. 1 = a contradiction or the same date three times.
6. **Simple English**: sentences under 20 words, one idea each, active voice, common words. 1 = sentences a non-native reader has to reread.
7. **Evidence bound**: a cause stated only when the artifact shows it. A recording that ends on a screen proves the person left; what the screen showed is a 1 when stated as fact. A closed bet's decision without its reason is a 2.
