# Weekly Update Rubric

How a weekly update is judged, by the manager and by a reviewing agent. Each criterion names the section of `weekly-format.md` it scores, 1–5, with quoted evidence. A check ends with seven scores and their quotes. A comparison ends with one table, drafts as columns, and the winning draft named with the criteria that decided it.

1. **Achievements, not process** (What goes in). 1 = the writer's own tests, things ruled out, "likely", "could not reproduce", or a ratio of done to planned ("eight of nine"). 5 = none of that, and no caveat that does not change the reader's conclusion.
2. **Three-minute read** (Shape, the word range). Count prose words, tables excluded. 5 = 500–700; 4 = 400–499 or 701–800; 3 = 801–900; 2 = 901–1,200; 1 = under 400 or over 1,200.
3. **Links once, meaning something** (Links). The same link repeated in table rows, a run of PR numbers, or a links row caps the score at 2.
4. **Order and blocks** (Shape, the five parts). 1 = What's next after the changelog, a fix inside a bet block, or an In short that is not a list of bets by goal. 2 = a closed bet's decision without its reason.
5. **Same facts everywhere** (Facts once). 1 = a contradiction between body and summary, or the same date three times.
6. **Simple English** (Language). 1 = sentences a non-native reader has to reread.
7. **Evidence bound** (the "State what is verified" rule in `../SKILL.md`). 1 = a cause the artifact does not show, stated as fact.
