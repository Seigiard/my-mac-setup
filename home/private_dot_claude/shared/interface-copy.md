# Interface copy: the grammar of a control

Reached from the interface-copy pointer in `~/.claude/CLAUDE.md`. Adapted from Bureau Gorbunov's syntax table for buttons. It refines `better-writing`: where that skill says every button starts with a verb, this table wins.

## Name a control by its job

First decide what the control does, then take the part of speech that job demands:

| Job | The label answers | Part of speech | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Command**: performs an action | What to do? | Verb in its base form, perfective in Russian | Buy, Send, Add, Save; Купить, Отправить, Сохранить |
| **Status**: the user states a fact about the object | What is it? What kind? How? | Noun, adjective, participle, number, adverb, or a first-person verb | This is spam, Approved, In progress, Later, I confirm; Это спам, Утверждено, Подтверждаю |
| **Navigation**: opens a place | What? | Noun in the nominative | Settings, Search, Fonts; Настройка, Поиск, Шрифты |

A label in another job's form promises that job: "Payment" on a button that charges the card reads as a link to a page. A command label that reads as a setting ("Save automatically"), an instruction ("Go back to the start of sign-up"), or a vague "More…" does not name one action.

The same intent can take more than one job. "Mark as spam" is the command, "This is spam" is the status; "Open settings" is the command, "Settings" is the place. Pick the job first, and the form follows from it.

## The action and its result share one word

A command's verb names its result too. The button "Publish" produces the toast "Published", and the record then carries the status "Published". The reader learns one word per action and recognises it at every step of the flow.
